# Credits and sources

## Project authorship

Alessandro Benevelli is the author of the project concept, visual direction, selection of artwork details, Italian anecdotes and helper texts. The prototype is coursework for COMP-2002 Web Development at Open Institute of Technology (OPIT), taught by Filip Biały.

The Italian source texts and the approved translations are preserved in the [multilingual archive](translations/caravaggio-anecdotes-and-helpers-8-languages.txt). This publication does not revise their wording.

## Paintings and reproductions

The paintings shown in the prototype are works by Michelangelo Merisi da Caravaggio. Artwork reproductions were sourced primarily through Wikipedia, as recorded in the author’s Practice Assignment 3. The crops were prepared in Figma.

Examples shown in this repository include *Basket of Fruit*, *David with the Head of Goliath*, and *Judith Beheading Holofernes*. Artwork captions in the prototype provide titles, dates and collection/location information.

A per-asset source and rights register remains to be completed before website media publication. This repository does not assign a blanket open-source licence to third-party reproductions or audiovisual material.

## Introductory audiovisual material

The author’s Practice Assignment 3 identifies documentary footage, particularly *Caravaggio — L’anima e il sangue* (2018), directed by Jesus Garces Lambert, Sky / Magnitudo Film, as source material for the introductory montage. It also identifies *Experience* by Ludovico Einaudi as the intended music.

The repository includes a screenshot of the prototype opening screen. Source video and music files are not distributed here. The credits above record the declared sources; they do not claim ownership of those works.

## Visual references

The design research recorded in Practice Assignment 3 cites Apple for section contrast, centred composition and negative space, and DATALAND for immersive video and horizontal exploration with side previews.

## Screenshot provenance

The README uses ten prototype preview screenshots, one Figma canvas overview and one close-up image supplied by Alessandro Benevelli on 25 October 2026. These files are reproduced without alterations. Their interface text retains the languages visible in the original captures.

Earlier exports from the project’s Figma nodes are also retained in the screenshot directory. Those gallery exports were cropped from their horizontal tracks to show viewport-sized compositions. No artwork, interface text or interaction was invented for these captures.
