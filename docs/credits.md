# Credits and sources

## Project authorship

Alessandro Benevelli is the author of the project concept, visual direction, selection of artwork details, Italian anecdotes and helper texts. The prototype is coursework for COMP-2002 Web Development at Open Institute of Technology (OPIT), taught by Filip Biały.

The Italian source texts and the approved translations are preserved in the [multilingual archive](translations/caravaggio-anecdotes-and-helpers-8-languages.txt). This publication does not revise their wording.

## Paintings and reproductions

The paintings shown in the prototype are works by Michelangelo Merisi da Caravaggio. Artwork reproductions were sourced primarily through Wikipedia, as recorded in the author’s Practice Assignment 3. The crops were prepared in Figma.

Examples shown in this repository include *Basket of Fruit*, *David with the Head of Goliath*, and *Judith Beheading Holofernes*. Artwork captions in the prototype provide titles, dates and collection/location information.

A per-asset source and rights register remains to be completed before website media publication. This repository does not assign a blanket open-source licence to third-party reproductions or audiovisual material.

The author’s contribution is selecting the excerpts, choosing their order and assembling the montage for this project. The cinematography and original film production are the work of the documentary’s credited creators.

## Visual references

**[Apple’s website](https://www.apple.com/it/) is a direct design reference recorded in Practice Assignment 3 and explicitly confirmed by the author.** The author liked its contrasting section backgrounds and centred imagery surrounded by empty space. These ideas were adapted to the LIGHT, SHADOW and BLOOD palettes and to the focused presentation of paintings and selected details.

The author connects this use of restrained backgrounds and empty space to Caravaggio’s concentration of attention on figures. This connection also helped motivate the choice of Caravaggio as the subject. The supplied Apple screenshot documents the visual reference; it is not a screen from the Caravaggio prototype. Apple’s website design and screenshot content belong to their respective owners.

**[dataland.art](https://dataland.art/), the website of DATALAND, the Museum of AI Arts in Los Angeles, is an explicit design inspiration identified by the author.** Two specific choices were adapted:

- **Near-full-screen opening video:** the author liked its immediate immersive impact and used a large introductory video to establish the atmosphere of the Caravaggio experience.
- **Central content with neighbouring items partially visible at the sides:** the author liked how this makes further horizontal content discoverable and adapted the idea to paintings, details and captions in each chapter.

The supplied DATALAND screenshots document these design references; they are not screens from the Caravaggio prototype. The DATALAND website design and screenshot content belong to their respective owners. Inspiration from its video presentation does not identify DATALAND as the source of the Caravaggio montage footage or music; those sources are credited separately above.
