# Credits and sources

## Project contribution

Conceived, designed and developed in 2026 by Alessandro Benevelli for COMP-2002 Web Development, taught by Dr. Filip Biały, as part of the Bachelor of Science (Honours) in Computer Science at Open Institute of Technology (OPIT).

Project work includes the concept, visual design, selection of painting details, original Italian anecdotes and explanations, video montage editing, and website implementation in HTML, CSS and JavaScript. This non-commercial university project was created for educational purposes and as a tribute to the art of Caravaggio.

The Italian source texts and their translations are preserved in the [multilingual archive](translations/caravaggio-anecdotes-and-helpers-8-languages.txt).

## Paintings and reproductions

The paintings shown on the website are works by Michelangelo Merisi da Caravaggio. Artwork reproductions were sourced primarily through Wikipedia, as recorded in Practice Assignment 3. The crops were prepared in Figma.

Examples shown in this repository include *Basket of Fruit*, *David with the Head of Goliath*, and *Judith Beheading Holofernes*. Artwork captions on the website provide titles, dates and collection/location information.

A per-asset source and rights register remains to be completed. This repository does not assign a blanket open-source licence to third-party reproductions or audiovisual material.

## Introductory audiovisual material

### How the project video was assembled

The introductory video was assembled by **selecting, trimming and joining scenes from *Caravaggio - L’anima e il sangue***, using **OpenShot Video Editor and OBS Studio**. The result is a re-edited montage for the Caravaggio website, accompanied by **“Experience” by Ludovico Einaudi**.

The editing involved selecting the excerpts, choosing their order and assembling the montage for this project. The cinematography and original film production are the work of the documentary’s credited creators.

## Visual references

**[Apple’s website](https://www.apple.com/it/) is a direct design reference recorded in Practice Assignment 3.** Its contrasting section backgrounds and centred imagery informed the LIGHT, SHADOW and BLOOD palettes and the presentation of paintings and selected details.

Restrained backgrounds and empty space connect this reference with Caravaggio’s concentration of attention on figures and informed the choice of subject. The Apple reference screenshot documents the visual inspiration; it is not a screen from the Caravaggio website. Apple’s website design and screenshot content belong to their respective owners.

**[dataland.art](https://dataland.art/), the website of DATALAND, the Museum of AI Arts in Los Angeles, was another source of design inspiration.** Two presentation choices were adapted.

- **Near-full-screen opening video:** A large introductory video establishes the atmosphere of the Caravaggio experience.
- **Central content with neighbouring items partially visible at the sides:** Applied to paintings, details and captions to make further horizontal content discoverable.

The DATALAND reference screenshots document these design inspirations; they are not screens from the Caravaggio website. The DATALAND website design and screenshot content belong to their respective owners. Inspiration from its video presentation does not identify DATALAND as the source of the Caravaggio montage footage or music; those sources are credited separately above.
